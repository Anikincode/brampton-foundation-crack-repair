Backwater Valve Installation in Brampton

If dirty water came up through your basement floor drain during a storm, that wasn't groundwater. It was the sewer. When the city sewer fills faster than it can drain, it pushes back up the pipe to the lowest opening in your house. A backwater valve closes that door. It's part of how we handle basement leak repair in Brampton.

Get a quote
Short answer

What does a backwater valve do? A backwater valve is a one-way valve installed on your home's main sewer pipe, usually under the basement floor. Wastewater flows out normally. When the city sewer backs up, a flap inside closes and stops sewage from coming back into the basement through floor drains, toilets and sinks.

Below: how to tell sewer backup from a leak, how the valve goes in, the Region of Peel rebate, living with a valve, and the cost.

How does a backwater valve go in?

  1. Permit first. A backwater valve needs a building permit from the City of Brampton before the work starts.
  2. Find the main line. The valve goes on the main sanitary pipe where it leaves the house, usually under the basement floor on the side facing the street.
  3. Open the floor. A section of concrete is cut out and dug down to the pipe.
  4. Install the valve. A section of pipe is cut out and the valve goes in its place, level and facing the right way.
  5. Inspection. A City building inspector checks the installation. Peel's rebate needs this approval.
  6. Close the floor. Concrete goes back around the valve, with an access cover so it can be checked and cleaned.

The Region of Peel backwater valve rebate

The Region of Peel pays back 60% of the invoice, up to $1,500 including tax, for a backwater valve on the sanitary line of a home in Peel, which includes Brampton. Per Peel's rebate page, read in October 2026:

  • Single, semi-detached and townhouses. Multi-unit homes and basement apartments that share a sewer pipe don't qualify.
  • The valve is on the sanitary line, not the storm line.
  • It was installed after July 16, 2024.
  • It has a City of Brampton building permit and passed inspection.
  • A licensed contractor installed it.
  • The homeowner applies as an individual, not a company.
  • One rebate per home every 10 years.

You apply after the valve passes inspection. On a $2,500 invoice, 60% is $1,500, so you'd get the full rebate. On a $3,500 invoice, 60% is $2,100, but the cap holds it at $1,500.

Peel also strongly recommends disconnecting downspouts from the sewer. If yours still connect, you'll be asked to sign a release form. Terms can change, so check Peel's backwater valve rebate page before you book.

What should you know about living with a valve?

  • When the valve is closed, nothing drains out. During a backup, hold off on laundry, showers and flushing until the storm passes. The flap that keeps the sewer out also keeps your own water in.
  • It needs checking. Open the access cover now and then, following the maker's instructions, and make sure the flap moves freely and nothing is caught in it.
  • It doesn't stop groundwater. Water through the walls or up through the slab needs a drainage fix, not a valve.

What does a backwater valve cost?

A 2026 GTA cost guide from RenoQuotes puts a backwater valve at about $2,000 to $4,500 before any rebate. Most of that is labour: cutting the floor, digging to the pipe and restoring the concrete.

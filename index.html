How do you fix a foundation crack? Most leaking foundation cracks are fixed from inside the basement by injecting the crack with polyurethane or epoxy resin. The resin fills the crack from the inside face of the wall to the soil side and seals it. Wide, moving or horizontal cracks need a structural check first.

On this page: what we repair, how a repair works step by step, what it costs, how to tell if your crack is serious, and where we work.

What foundation cracks do we repair?

Leaking wall cracks

The most common call. Water comes through a vertical or diagonal crack after rain or snow melt. Usually sealed from inside with crack injection.

Dry cracks before you finish the basement

No water yet, but drywall is about to go up. Sealing a crack now costs far less than opening a finished wall later.

Cracks that need outside work

Block walls, cracks behind stairs or built-ins, or failed outside waterproofing. See exterior foundation crack repair.

Basement and garage floor cracks

Most are harmless shrinkage. The ones that let water up are worth sealing. See floor crack repair.

Leaking tie-rod holes

Rusty spots in a grid on a poured wall, seeping after rain. Sealed the same way as cracks: tie-rod hole leak repair.

Crumbling parging

The grey coating on the outside of the foundation cracking or falling off. See parging repair.

How does a crack repair work?

Think of it like filling a cavity. You don't paint over the hole. You clean it out and fill it all the way through, so nothing gets back in.

  1. Look at the crack first. Width, direction, whether it leaks, and whether one side sits out from the other. That decides the fix.
  2. Clean and set the ports. The crack surface gets cleaned and small injection ports go on every few inches along it.
  3. Inject from the bottom up. Resin goes in at low pressure, starting at the lowest port, until it shows at the next port up.
  4. Cure and finish. Once the resin sets, the ports are trimmed off and the surface is tidied up.
  5. Paperwork. You get a written record of the repair and a 10-year warranty against water coming through the repaired crack. It transfers to the next owner if you sell.

Most single-crack interior repairs take a few hours, not days. The full walkthrough is on our foundation crack injection page.

Is your crack serious?

Not every crack is a problem. Thin vertical cracks in poured concrete are often just shrinkage from when the concrete cured. Horizontal cracks, wide cracks and cracks that keep growing are the ones to take seriously.

Questions people ask before booking

Can I fix a foundation crack myself?

DIY kits exist for thin, dry cracks in poured concrete. They're harder to get right on a crack that's actively leaking, and they don't work on concrete block walls. If water is coming in, a professional injection is the safer bet.

Does home insurance cover foundation cracks?

Usually not when the crack comes from settling or shrinkage. Policies differ, so check yours before you assume either way.

My house is under two years old. Who pays?

In Ontario, water coming through the basement or foundation walls is covered by the Tarion two-year warranty on new homes. Call your builder first. Dampness alone isn't covered, but actual water leaking in is. See Tarion's coverage page.

Can cracks be repaired in winter?

Interior injection can be done any time of year because the work happens inside the basement. Exterior digging depends on ground conditions.

How long does it take?

Most single-crack interior repairs take a few hours.
